# v6.5.0 "Anselmo"

Faster Midstate Walk mining on CPUs, NVIDIA GPUs that mine it, and an
AxeOS-style dashboard with settings for `midstate miner`. No consensus change:
a node on v6.2.0 or later follows the same chain through the switch to
Midstate Walk at block **371,500**. Miners should upgrade, and anyone with an
NVIDIA GPU must, to mine the Walk on it.

## Midstate Walk on NVIDIA GPUs

The CUDA miner in v6.3.0 and v6.4.0 computed every walk wrong, on every
NVIDIA GPU. Each card fails the known answers when the miner opens it, so it
is refused ("got a known answer wrong") and the miner mines on the CPU. No
wrong work is ever mined, but from block 371,500 those versions mine the Walk
on the CPU alone.

The GPU code ships as PTX, compiled ahead of time with clang. Clang 18
compiled the walk's 64-bit rotate to two 64-bit shifts without first reducing
the amount mod 64, and PTX turns a shift of 64 or more into zero, so the
rotate came out 0 almost every time. The kernels now build the rotate from
32-bit shifts. CI now runs the compiled PTX itself on a CPU, translated
instruction by instruction with PTX's rules, against the known answers. The
broken PTX gives exactly the wrong hash the first GPU reported, and the fixed
one gives every known answer. A Quadro M6000 mines at 819 attempts/s.

## A faster CPU miner

A Walk step spends most of its time waiting for its read from the 256 KiB
pad. A CPU thread can step walks of other nonces in turn and fill those
waits, as long as their pads still fit the core's caches. Whether they do
depends on the CPU, so the miner measures it:

- Before its first Walk job, the CPU miner times 1 to 4 walks per thread on
  all its threads (about 6 s, once per run). It checks the known answers that
  way, mines with the fastest, and logs the rates:
  `Midstate Walk: attempts with 1 to 4 walks per thread on 12 threads: 1: 1630/s, 2: 1793/s, 3: 1645/s, 4: 1355/s; mining with 2`
- `MINER_WALKS_PER_THREAD=1` to `4` sets it instead.
- With one walk per thread, the step itself is written faster: about 5% on
  a Raspberry Pi 5 and on x86.

| CPU | Walks per thread chosen | Against v6.4.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Raspberry Pi 5, 4 threads | 1 | about +3.5% (1,490 to 1,542 attempts/s) |
| Xeon E5-1650 v3, 12 threads (256 KiB of L2 a core) | 2 | about +10% over one walk per thread |
| A 2-core cloud Xeon (2 MiB of L2 a core) | 3 | +89% (about 600 to 1,132 attempts/s) |

The more cache a core has, the more it gains. Zen 4 and Zen 5 cores have
1 MiB of L2, and should gain too; none has been measured here yet.

Each hash is exactly its nonce's walk. Every way of writing the step, at 1 to
4 walks per thread, is tested against the known answers in every slot, and
against the reference step over 6,000 random programs.

`midstate miner`, `midstate node --mine`, the desktop wallet's miner and the
CUDA miner's CPU threads all mine this way. `midstate bench-pow` measures
with the miner's choice, and `bench-pow --interleave` shows every count.

## `midstate miner`: a dashboard and settings like a Bitaxe's

The miner has its own page, laid out as AxeOS is:

- **Status:** hashrate over a minute, ten minutes and an hour; attempts per
  joule; shares accepted and rejected; and the best share.
- **Charts:** hashrate and temperature over 15 minutes to 24 hours.
- **Board:** power (a Pi 5's rails), heat against the 80 and 85 °C limits,
  and the fan.
- **Pool and system:** the pool connection, and the system, with the last
  500 log lines.
- **Alerts:** a line across the top says when something needs you: no pool,
  no new job for three minutes, or under-voltage.

A **Settings** tab changes the miner while it runs:

- **Mining:** the pool and a fallback pool, payout address, worker, midwimble
  address, threads and a heat limit, kept in `miner-settings.toml`.
- **On a Raspberry Pi:** the clock and extra voltage, from the board's presets
  and limits, and on a Pi 5 the fan curve. These take effect at the next
  boot. Run `sudo midstate board-setup` once to let the miner set them.
- **Test this unit,** and restart the Pi.
- **Who can change them:** anyone on the local network, as on a Bitaxe, and
  never another site's page.

The stratum client fails over to `--fallback-pool-url` after three failed
tries, and goes back once the pool answers. It gives up connecting after ten
seconds, leaves a pool that has been silent for five minutes, and waits for
new settings after a refused login instead of exiting. `--heat-limit` sheds
a thread every 30 seconds while the board is at the limit. See MANUAL.md,
section 6.

## Benchmarks

- `midstate bench-pow --gpu` runs the Walk on any GPU through wgpu (Vulkan,
  Metal, DX12) and only measures; the miner does not use it. On a Pi 5's GPU
  it does 13 attempts/s at best, under 1% of the Pi's CPU, so the Pi mines on
  its CPU.
- `tools/walk-bench` holds the Walk's benchmarks without the rest of midstate
  and builds in seconds. `midstate-walk-bench cpu-probe` shows where a step's
  time goes on this CPU, in cycles.

## Upgrading

- **NVIDIA GPU miners:** upgrade to mine the Walk on your GPUs.
- **Other miners, and the desktop wallet's miner:** upgrade for the speed.
- **`midstate miner` on a Pi:** upgrade for the dashboard and its settings.
- **Pools:** optional. A pool's midwimble node gets the same miner fixes in
  midwimble v1.3.2-DIMEBAG.
- **Everyone else:** optional. v6.2.0 and later follow the same chain.
