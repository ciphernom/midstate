Five throws of ten dice.
The rest is arithmetic.

Every bit of your seed phrase comes from your hands. Ten dice thrown together carry 25.8 bits, so a 12-word phrase takes five throws and a 24-word phrase takes ten. This page converts what you rolled into words and appends the checksum bits no one can compute by hand.

01Phrase length

128 bits already exceeds what any attacker can search. Choose 24 words if you doubt the fairness of your dice — the extra margin absorbs it.

02What you are throwing

Dice in one throw

This only sets the countdown. Throwing more dice at once is what makes the method quick — the dice do not have to be different colours, but they do have to be read where they land.

03Record it

0 of 128 bits

Type every throw in the order it happened, one throw per line if you like. Never re-roll one you dislike and never delete one to "fix" a pattern — a fair sequence is allowed to look strange.

04Second source

0 of 128 bits

Pick something that fails differently: cards depend on a shuffle, dice on a throw. Two sets of dice from the same bag share whatever is wrong with the bag.

How to run this safely
  • Get the page off the network. Save this file, move it to a machine with radios off and cable out, and open it from disk. A live browser tab is the one thing standing between your entropy and the world.
  • Roll where nothing is watching. No phones on the table, no cameras, no windows behind the screen. Throw into a tray or a box lid so nothing escapes.
  • Read the dice where they lie — left to right, top to bottom, nearest row first. Never tidy them into order by their numbers.
  • Copy the words onto paper by hand. Do not use the clipboard. This page has no copy button on purpose.
  • Destroy the sheet you recorded throws on. That sheet is your seed phrase in another alphabet. Burn or shred it.
  • There is no second-entry check, on purpose. Mistyping a throw gives you a different random seed, not a broken one — what matters is copying the words correctly, so spend the care there.
  • Restore before you fund. Enter the words into your wallet, confirm it accepts them, send a small amount, wipe the device, restore from paper, send it back out. Only then move real money.
  • Close the tab and power the machine down when you are done.
What this page can and cannot do

It cannot make randomness. Math.random is deliberately replaced with a function that throws an exception, so any hidden attempt to invent entropy crashes the page instead of quietly seeding your wallet.

It cannot phone home. No fonts, scripts, images or styles are loaded from anywhere — which is also why the typography falls back to your system faces if Inter and JetBrains Mono are not installed locally. A content policy in the page header forbids every outbound connection type. Nothing is written to storage or cookies, so the theme toggle lasts only as long as the tab.

It cannot let you skip the arithmetic. SHA-256 is the one step of BIP-39 that no one can do at a kitchen table. The checksum is four bits for a 12-word phrase and eight for a 24-word phrase — the dashed cells at the end of the ledger. Everything else on screen came from your throws.

You still have to trust this code. That is unavoidable for the checksum step, so the page tests itself against the official BIP-39 reference vectors on every load and reports the result in the header. If you want a second opinion, take the entropy shown on the card to any independent BIP-39 tool, offline, and check that it produces the same words.

Hashing, bias, and why two sources settle it

A hash is a concentrator, not a source. SHA-256 gathers whatever unpredictability is already in your throws into a uniform 256 bits. It cannot create any. Folding 130 bits of dice into a 128-bit seed therefore buys nothing at all, which is why the fold option here demands twice the target before it will build.

Fixed bias is the easy case. A die that shows one face 30% of the time still carries about 2.4 bits. A coin biased to 51% loses roughly three hundredths of a bit per flip. Hash a generous surplus and none of that survives.

Correlation is the hard case. Min-entropy per throw is not min-entropy of the sequence. A tower with a favoured exit, a practised controlled throw, dice that stick together, a deck that was never properly mixed — each roll looks fine on its own while the record as a whole is far more predictable than the count suggests. Per-throw arithmetic quietly assumes independence, and no amount of hashing repairs a dependence you did not notice.

So do not argue about it — route around it. Fold two records made by unrelated physical mechanisms and the result is sound if either one was honest. A flaw in your dice says nothing about your shuffle. That is why this page asks each source for the full target on its own: either one alone would have been enough, and you no longer need to know which one was.

The trade you are making. Folding is the safer default and it is opaque: the ledger becomes a hash output with no visible relationship to your throws. Direct extraction is transparent and hand-checkable, and it passes any bias in your dice straight through. Precision casino dice are worth buying if you intend to rely on dice alone.

Why the method is built this way

Entropy per action, not per roll. One die rolled 128 times and ten dice thrown 13 times produce the same randomness, but one is a chore and chores make people cut corners. Ten six-sided dice in a single throw carry 25.8 bits. Fifty dice cover a 12-word phrase, a hundred cover a 24-word phrase.

Identical dice are fine. Sorting them is not. Where each die lands is independent of what it shows, so reading a scattered handful left to right gives you a genuinely random ordering and the full 6n outcomes. Arranging them by value throws that away: ten sorted dice are worth 11.5 bits instead of 25.8, a loss of more than half. If two dice are hard to order, break the tie by position — never by the number on top.

A cocked die has no value yet, so re-rolling that one die cannot depend on what it shows and costs nothing. Re-rolling a die because you dislike its number is a rule that depends on the number, and that is the difference between a fair sample and a chosen one.

No modulo, anywhere. Turning dice into a big number and reducing it modulo 2048 is the standard way this goes quietly wrong, because no power of six is divisible by 2048. Instead the page treats everything you type as one growing uniform number and peels off whole bits only once they are certain, carrying the leftover uncertainty into the next throw. It recovers 2.5848 bits per die against a theoretical 2.5850, and the page proves the result is unbiased across all 216 three-dice throws on every load.

One pipeline, any source. Because each entry carries its own number of outcomes, dice, cards and coins all feed the same extractor. A card is just a digit whose base shrinks as the deck empties — which is also why dealing a card twice is caught immediately.

Cards are the fastest source you already own, and the weakest link is the shuffle rather than the arithmetic. A wash followed by seven riffles and a cut is the accepted standard; a fresh deck out of the box is in a known order and is worth nothing until it is properly mixed.
